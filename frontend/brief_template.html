RosterWatch

A first pass at both phases of the plan, built on free nflverse and Sleeper data. Phase 1 catches sudden usage shifts. A Phase 2 prototype catches the slow bleed. Each detector's threshold was tested on four past seasons. All of it is a starting point for us to argue about.

Phase 2 preview · the slow bleed

Would RosterWatch have caught it in 2025?

Two 2025 backfields match the problem in our overview: a veteran slowly giving up work to a younger back. The lines show weekly snap share. The markers show the first week each player's slow-bleed or slow-climb tally crossed the flag line.

Houston had an earlier sign too. In Week 4, Marks already had 44% of the team's passing-down targets and carries (3rd/4th down and the last two minutes of a half), while Chubb still played 42% of snaps. Passing-down work moved before overall snaps did.

Two detectors

Each flag traces back to a few averages and a noise estimate measured on 2022–25. There is no black box.

Phase 1 · Shift score

Sudden changes

Average of the last 2 games minus the average of earlier games this season, divided by how much usage normally moves for that position. +2 means the change was twice normal noise.

(mean last 2 − mean earlier) / σpos
Phase 2 · CUSUM tally

Slow bleeds and climbs

Sets a player's established level from his first 3 games, then keeps a running total of how far each game lands from it. Small misses add up. One outlier game counts for at most 3 SD.

tally = min(0, tally + zgame + 0.5)
Inputs

What counts as usage

Snap share, opportunity share (share of team targets + carries) and passing-down share. All three are volume measures, so one long touchdown can't create a flag.

opp = (tgt + car) / team(tgt + car)

Defending the thresholds

Both detectors ran on every RB/WR/TE game from 2022–2025, and we checked the next 3 games. A higher bar means fewer flags, and more of them hold up. The prototype's settings are highlighted.

Shift score (rises)

"Stayed up": next-3-game usage still above the old baseline.

CUSUM tally

Hit: next-3-game snap share at least 5 points past the established level, in the flagged direction. The reference row shows how often that happens with no signal.

Ideas to take further

phase 2

Volume vs. one big play

Score how much of a player's fantasy output came from a single play, and show it next to each flag. A 60-yard touchdown on 3 touches should never look like a role.

phase 2

Earned vs. inherited roles

Use Sleeper and nflverse injury data to label each rise as earned or as covering for an injured starter. Then backtest what happens when the starter returns.

phase 2

Does passing-down share lead?

Test the Houston pattern across all backfields. Does a drop in passing-down share predict a later drop in total snaps, and by how many weeks?

statistics

Better established levels

Use last season's role as a prior for a player's established level, shrunk toward his early 2026 games. Then CUSUM works from Week 1.

data

Routes and personnel

For 2016–2025, per-play on-field data gives routes run and 3rd-down snaps directly. That's useful for validating the play-by-play estimate.

product

Market lag

Snapshot Sleeper adds and drops every week and measure how many weeks the market trails our flags. That gap is the tool's value.
